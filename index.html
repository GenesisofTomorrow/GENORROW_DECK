I · The Thesis
01 —

The Problem We Own

Autonomous AI systems are being deployed into critical environments — defense, aerospace, contested networks — with a foundational unresolved question at their core: how do you verify the identity and output integrity of an agent that is, by design, non-deterministic?

The black-box nature of autonomous AI in high-uncertainty, mission-critical environments introduces a terminal trust gap. We work the R&D at this junction — constructing quantum-hybrid frameworks that can be directly implemented into existing critical pipelines.

Classical cryptographic methods — no matter how sophisticated — are predicated on computational hardness assumptions that are increasingly fragile against quantum adversaries. Grover's algorithm reduces 256-bit hash security to 128-bit equivalence. Shor's dissolves RSA and ECC entirely. The timeline is not theoretical. The horizon may have already arrived.



GENORROW's answer is not to patch the classical paradigm. It is to replace the foundation — using quantum mechanics itself as the source of security. Unpredictability grounded in physical indeterminacy, not computational limitation.

Active Research Projects

Project I Springer Nature · Published
MARK-B.L.U.
Base Layer Unification — Quantum Hash Function Framework for Agent Identity & Output Verification

A dynamically evolving quantum security architecture designed for autonomous AI in defense and critical infrastructure. The system generates quantum-native cryptographic identity signatures that shapeshift with each timeslot — making autonomous agent identity as ephemeral as a wavefunction, and as verifiable as a law of physics.

The architecture operates through a 16-qubit, 6-layer parameterized quantum circuit with brick-wall CNOT entanglement, SHA-512 pre-stretching, and SHAKE-256 amplitude extraction — achieving a 256-bit quantum hash digest. Empirically validated against entropy, collision resistance, avalanche effect, bit independence, and Hamming distance benchmarks — placing it in quantitative competition with production-grade classical hash functions.

Project II NeurIPS 2026 · Submitted
PAST-QAFTROM-X
Phase-Structured Quantum Fourier Transform with Optimised Modulation & eXpressiveness

A quantum kernel learning architecture that resolves three simultaneous pathologies of existing quantum feature maps: degenerate expressiveness, exponential gradient vanishing (barren plateaus), and the absence of principled task-adaptive kernel optimisation.

A single trainable diagonal phase operator inserted before the Quantum Fourier Transform yields N real learnable parameters — provably avoiding barren plateaus with gradient magnitude scaling as Ω(N⁻¹/²) (polynomial, not exponential). At N=2¹⁶, PAST-QAFTROM-X gradients are 14 million times larger than generic parameterised quantum circuit baselines, which become completely untrainable at that scale.
